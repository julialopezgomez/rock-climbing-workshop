\section{Input, output and geometry}
The image challenge provides a stereo RGB pair. The estimator returns each
hold's type, color, backplate-anchor position in metres and intrinsic XYZ Euler
orientation in radians. The scene frame has its origin at the wall's bottom
centre: x points right, y up and z out. A mesh centre is different from its
backplate anchor. These conventions determine the submission coordinates.

The updated public source supports Cube, Microban, Sidepull and Fingerpocket.
We pin the four meshes and scene geometry to source commit
\href{https://github.com/jackybaltes/rcr-perception/tree/51efe48b9765a0de7c3a4658a8b7bb781faf3459}{\texttt{51efe48}}.
The supplied sample rig has a 3\,m by 1.4\,m wall, a 64\,mm stereo baseline,
1\,m camera distance, 0.7\,m camera height and 74\textdegree\ vertical field of
view. Each stereo panel is 699 by 318 pixels. These are declared inputs;
labelled hold positions are never used to estimate camera calibration.

Wall width also changes the tetrahedral volumes. Their base height is
\(\min(0.1w,h)\), where \(w,h\) are the wall dimensions. The supplied wall
therefore uses a 0.3\,m base height. The former 0.8\,m wall and 0.08\,m volume
assumptions displaced inclined support faces. We now use the declared wall
size throughout rendering, fitting and JSON export.

\section{Selected hold estimator}
\paragraph{Find and pair instances.}
Color components initialize hold proposals. Epipolar consistency and projection
onto the known wall pair observations across cameras. The old 100\,mm clearance
merge and narrow-instance gate were removed: the new scenes contain closer
instances and wider Sidepull holds. Proposals still depend on the released
rendered appearance.

\paragraph{Fit all four meshes in both images.}
For each proposal, the estimator renders all four normalized CAD meshes at the
three public in-plane yaw choices: zero and plus/minus 45\textdegree.
Candidate support surfaces are the wall and image-estimated volume faces.
MuJoCo renders their silhouettes and occlusions; local optimization fits the
candidate to both views. Promising inclined candidates receive translation and
pose refinement. Flat face regions in RGB estimate the volumes, with hold
pixels excluded from face refinement.

Fingerpocket has weak silhouette differences between yaw choices. A second
check compares stereo RGB correspondences predicted by each candidate's
rendered depth. This is CAD hypothesis depth, not a depth-camera measurement.
The check chooses between close silhouette fits only when the RGB evidence
has a sufficient gap.

\paragraph{Recover a compatible scene.}
The previous ten-Microban generator search is removed. The replacement uses
estimated geometry: align each volume-mounted backplate to its support face,
apply the source's approximately 1.73\,mm clearance, and check quantized convex
hulls for overlap. The weaker image fit moves tangentially to its support plane
when a conflict occurs. Unresolved candidates are rejected. Diagnostics record
all changes. This stage does not recover a random seed or claim exact source
JSON.

\section{Results and remaining errors}
We evaluated all 40 supplied scenes, containing 1,200 holds. Matching requires
the correct type and position error at most 50\,mm. A strict pose additionally
requires position error below 5\,mm and rotation error below 15\textdegree,
measured on SO(3). Missed holds remain in the denominator. These are local
diagnostics; the repository does not supply an official weighted scorer.

Four scenes guided image-fit development. We froze the image-fitting files
before evaluating the remaining 36: these gave 1,041/1,080 matches and 896 strict
poses. The source-clearance correction was added after observing scene-validator
failures on the full set. Table~\ref{tab:results} therefore reports final
regression results, not a fresh blind evaluation.

Orientation is the main remaining error. Among matched holds, 141 are within
5\,mm but fail the rotation check. Table~\ref{tab:types} shows that Fingerpocket
accounts for most strict-pose failures. At this image resolution, its similar
yaw appearances remain ambiguous after silhouette and RGB refinement.
Thirteen matched holds exceed 5\,mm position error. Partial visibility, missed
components and uncertain volume faces also remain relevant; the current
experiments do not isolate their individual contributions.

The supplied generator's random type list currently excludes Cube, despite
supporting its mesh. We generated three additional controls by extending only
that list. Their renderer, collision rules and volume geometry were unchanged.
Across 48 holds, the estimator gives 47 matches and 37 strict poses; the 12 Cube
instances give 11 matches and 10 strict poses. All three scene envelopes pass
the upstream validator. These controls are separate from the supplied data.

\paragraph{What earlier trials established.}
The initial mixed-type pilot retained CAD fitting because it produced 51/60
matches and 45/60 strict poses on two scenes. None of its five volume-mounted
holds passed the strict check, motivating the geometry update. The legacy
sampler's assumptions were incompatible with the new source. Earlier
FoundationPose, SAM-6D and stereo-network experiments remain on the full branch;
they do not supply a 40-scene comparison against this updated estimator.
Multiview CAD remains the deployed method. The new evaluation establishes its
current performance, not superiority over unrun alternatives.

\section{Run and inspect the implementation}
Hold inference needs Linux x86-64, Python 3.12 and functioning EGL/OpenGL
rendering. It does not require Torch or a CUDA compiler. Setup downloads the
four pinned, checksum-verified meshes into a private cache.
\begin{verbatim}
python3.12 scripts/perception/setup.py
source .venv-perception/bin/activate
python scripts/perception/run.py doctor
python scripts/perception/run.py reproduce \
  --workers 2 --out outputs/perception/holds
\end{verbatim}
The two retained fixtures contain 37 holds. A fresh environment and empty cache
completed installation and reproduction: 36 matches, 31 strict poses, no false
positives, and both exported scenes accepted by the upstream validator.
A completed command does not imply that all poses passed.

For the supplied archives, use the declared sample rig:
\begin{verbatim}
python scripts/perception/prepare_samples.py \
  --samples samples --out data/local/perception_samples
python scripts/perception/run.py reproduce \
  --dataset data/local/perception_samples --workers 4 \
  --out outputs/perception/samples
\end{verbatim}
For another rig, supply measured calibration. A new pair uses
\texttt{holds --stereo stereo.png --calibration rig.json --out DIR}.
GPU jobs wait for spare capacity and poll every 30 minutes while externally busy.

\section{Robot pose and physical cameras}
Robot implementations are unchanged. Saved-frame \texttt{max} uses Microban
keypoints, measured-joint configured M3T and protected CAD/boundary refinement.
Continuous \texttt{fast} adds persistent tracking, RGB cup mapping and conditional
socket correction. Both require all 19 measured joints. Add
\texttt{setup.py --robot} for their dependencies and \texttt{--cameras} for
color-only RealSense capture.

The retained robot fixture passes root and endpoint checks: 0.879\,mm translation,
0.297\textdegree\ rotation and 1.759\,mm worst endpoint error. Combined replay
returns a valid root pose and 72 cup candidates without a refresh error;
candidate count alone is not map accuracy. Shared team types, submodules,
planning and controller code are unchanged.

The guide gives capture and chessboard-calibration commands for two selected
camera serials. Measure the square size and calibrate the actual resolution,
serial order and capture profile. Estimate wall pose separately. Encoder
acquisition times must share the camera host's monotonic clock. No physical
cameras were connected: USB capture, exposure synchronization and physical
accuracy remain unvalidated.

Keep challenge backplate JSON separate from the physical cup map and team bolt
coordinates. Socket proximity never confirms loading. The next camera test
must measure calibration and pose errors on the actual rig. The rendered-scene
result supports the four-type CAD path; small Fingerpocket images still leave
substantial orientation ambiguity.
